# agent instruction

**Ask a subagent what you got wrong, not whether you are right.** "Every research or review brief must ask explicitly for contradictions, for what the existing record gets wrong, and for anything that cuts against the position you just stated — and must say not to soften it in either direction. A brief that asks for confirmation gets confirmation."

*Grounded in: the three highest-value findings of this session all arrived under a 'what does this contradict?' heading.*

# justification

Every brief in this session carried a section asking what the source contradicted and what the existing note had got wrong. That section returned: a source note whose central claim was inverted relative to the paper, a headline statistic that turned out to be four unrepeated sessions with no statistics, and a framing error of the orchestrator's own that had already been committed. None of those would have surfaced under "summarise this source". One brief went further and named the owner's stated position with the instruction to say plainly if the source cut against it — and the reader returned exactly that, which became a correction the owner acted on. The marginal cost is three sentences per brief. The cost of omitting them is a research pass that reliably confirms whatever the orchestrator already believed.
